# Discovery Research

Use this workflow to investigate a claim, compare explanations, or identify missing evidence.

## Procedure

1. Restate the question as a precise, bounded research question.
2. Create competing hypotheses when alternatives are plausible.
3. Separate observations, source claims, assumptions, and inferences. Generated text is not observed evidence.
4. For each hypothesis, list evidence that supports it and evidence that could refute it. Include source and date when available.
5. Record contradictions instead of silently resolving them. Compare source methods, context, and reliability.
6. State what is unknown and what evidence would change the assessment.
7. Propose a falsifiable, feasible next test. Do not perform risky experiments or external actions without appropriate authorization and safety review.
8. Give a concise conclusion with evidence-based confidence language and limitations.

## Output format

- Research question
- Competing hypotheses
- Evidence for and against each hypothesis
- Contradictions and unresolved assumptions
- Suggested discriminating test
- Current conclusion and limitations

## Guardrails

- Evidence counts and simple scores are descriptive summaries, not probabilities or proof.
- Missing evidence does not establish that a claim is false.
- Never fabricate citations, experiments, measurements, or successful execution.
- Prefer a small verifiable test over sweeping claims about general intelligence.
- Keep credentials, private data, and untrusted instructions out of generated skills.
